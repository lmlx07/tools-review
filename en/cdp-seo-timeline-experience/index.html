New Site SEO: Expectation Management from Submission to Indexing

Created2026-06-09|Updated2026-09-11|SEO PracticeExperience Sharing
|Post Views:

Summary in one sentence: New site indexing timeline: API submission → 13 days for first crawl → 37 days for indexing → 1~4 weeks for stable search appearance. No movement in the first two weeks is normal; no indexing after a month means it’s time to investigate.

Complete Timeline

From URL submission to stable search result appearance, the journey roughly goes through these phases:

Note:

  • This is the normal case timeline; new sites may take longer
  • Unlicensed GitHub Pages sites may be slower than domestically-hosted sites
  • Submission does not guarantee indexing — search engines have their own quality standards

Phase Details

Phase 1: First Crawl (Days 1~3)

After submission, the search engine adds the URL to its crawl queue. The spider waits in the queue for its turn to visit.

Things you CAN confirm at this stage:

  • ✅ Whether submission succeeded (API returns success > 0)
  • ❌ Cannot confirm if the spider has actually visited

Things that are pointless at this stage:

  • ❌ Frequently searching site:domain.com — too early, definitely no results
  • ❌ Repeatedly submitting the same URLs — wastes quota

Phase 2: Entering the Index (Days 3~7)

After the crawler fetches the page content, the search engine performs quality assessment:

Factors affecting indexing:

  • Whether the page has substantial content (not an empty page)
  • Whether content is original (not scraped/copied)
  • Page load speed
  • Mobile-friendliness

How to check: Look at the “Regular Indexing” page in the webmaster platform to see index status.

Phase 3: Stable Search Result Appearance (Weeks 1~4)

Even if a page is indexed, it doesn’t mean it will rank high in search results. New sites typically start from very low positions:

Important to understand: Search result ranking depends on content quality, external links, site authority, and many other factors. Indexing ≠ Ranking.

Factors Affecting Indexing Speed

Factor Faster Slower
Domain age Aged domains (>1 year) Newly registered domains
Hosting platform Domestic filed hosting GitHub Pages and other foreign platforms
Content quality Original, valuable, frequently updated Scraped, low-quality, rarely updated
External links Links from authoritative sites No external links at all
Submission method API > Manual No submission, waiting for crawler discovery
Site structure Clear sitemap + robots.txt Messy link structure

How to Check Your Current Progress

Method 1: site: Search

  • Has results → At least some pages are indexed
  • No results → Still waiting, or not indexed

Example:

Method 2: Webmaster Platform

Log into the webmaster platform to check:

  • Regular Indexing → Look at the “indexed” count
  • Crawl Diagnostics → Test whether a single URL is crawlable

Method 3: Log Analysis

If you can access server logs (GitHub Pages doesn’t provide this), you can see crawler visit records.

Reasonable Expectations by Time

Time Reasonable Expectation Unreasonable Expectation
Day 1 Submission succeeded site: shows results (impossible this fast)
Week 1 Crawl may have happened Stable top-page ranking
Week 2 Some pages may be indexed Keywords all have rankings
Month 1 Sporadic search results start appearing Hundreds of daily visitors
Month 3 Steady amount of indexed content Site is an industry authority

When to Worry

Normal (Don’t Panic)

Abnormal (Investigate)

How to Accelerate Indexing

1. Keep Producing Content

Search engines favor frequently updated sites. Publishing 1~2 articles per week is more effective than publishing 50 at once.

2. External Link Building

Publish content on these platforms with links back to your site:

  • Medium / Dev.to (for technical articles in English)
  • GitHub (add links in project READMEs)
  • Stack Overflow (reference your articles when answering questions)
  • Twitter / LinkedIn (share article links)

Search engines sometimes discover new sites through external links faster than through manual submission.

3. Good Internal Link Structure

Add “related articles” recommendations at the bottom of each article to help crawlers navigate within your site.

4. Optimize robots.txt and sitemap

Ensure the sitemap only includes pages you want indexed — exclude private pages, tag pages, etc.

Summary

Phase Time What to Do
Early (0~3 days) After submission Don’t keep site: searching, it’s useless
Mid (1~4 weeks) Waiting for indexing Keep producing content, build external links
Late (1~3 months) Stabilization Check indexing data, optimize content quality
Long-term (3+ months) Growth phase Build links, deepen content

Mindset management matters more than technical operations — SEO is a “delayed feedback” process. What you do today won’t show results for a week or even a month. Patience + consistent output = eventual indexing.

Summary: SEO isn’t magic — it’s about helping search engines better understand your site. Submitting URLs is just the first knock on the door; content quality and consistent updates are what keep you inside. Remember the timeline — no movement for a week is normal, no movement for a month means start investigating, and no movement for three months means it’s time to change strategy.
